Sunburn damages both leaves and fruit on pineapple when high radiation combines with drought, low humidity, or reduced canopy cover. Fruit sunburn is the yield-critical form: it downgrades fresh-market fruit and creates entry points for rots. Sunburn is not a disease but is often mistaken for one.

**Regional priority:** High on exposed sites, thin canopies, ratoon blocks (uneven cover), and any block with fruit exposed to direct afternoon sun.

## When pineapple is most vulnerable

Most damaging during **fruit development and ripening (months 14 to 22)**, especially the west-facing rows and outer rows. Also affects immature plants during establishment on BRIS or open sites.

## How sunburn shows in satellite data

- Not directly detectable at Sentinel scale in most cases; damage is on individual fruit shoulders.
- On drone or very-high-resolution imagery, bleached leaves show as brighter reflectance on west-facing rows.
- **Indirect indicator**: sustained high solar radiation \+ low NDWI over the block during fruit development is a sunburn risk signal.
- Confirm on the ground.

## Field symptoms

- **Bleached, papery patches** on leaves exposed to the sun (west or south side)
- **Sun-scalded fruit shoulders** - the upper, sun-facing side of the fruit turns pale, then brown, then leathery
- **Cracked fruit skin** on the scalded area, often followed by [black rot](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/black-rot)
- **Corky, tough flesh** below the scalded skin
- Damage concentrated on **west-facing rows and outer rows**

## Reduce the damage by

- **Cover fruit with dried leaves** tied over the fruit shoulders (traditional method, effective and cheap).
- **Use paper or fabric fruit covers** on export-quality MD2 and Josapine.
- **Plant rows north-south** where possible; east-west rows expose fruit to direct afternoon sun.
- **Maintain full canopy** - avoid excessive N late in the cycle that causes older leaves to lean out and expose fruit.
- **Kaolin-clay spray** on fruit shoulders reduces radiation load on high-value blocks.
- **Time forcing** so fruit development avoids the hottest, driest weeks of the year where possible.
- **Irrigate during heat spells** - well-watered plants transpire and cool the fruit; dry plants overheat.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Sunburn risk correlates with radiation and evaporation, not NDVI, so use weather and canopy-cover signals.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for sunburn on pineapple:

- **Weather alert** - 3 or more consecutive days with maximum air temperature above 34 C and solar radiation above 25 MJ/m2/day during fruit development.
- **Canopy-cover alert** - trigger on blocks with NDVI below the mid-fruit-development baseline (indicates exposed fruit).
- **Fruit-cover reminder** - schedule a walk-through 2 weeks after forcing to install fruit covers on all west-facing rows.
