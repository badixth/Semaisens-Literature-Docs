Pineapple is a 14 to 24 month crop planted at 50,000 to 70,000 plants per hectare on plastic mulch. Diagnosis works at three scales at once: whole block, planting bed, and individual plant. Use this guide to move from a field observation or a satellite alert to a specific cause and a specific action.

For the standard census and inspection sequence a scout should follow on the ground, see [Field Walk Protocol](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/field-walk-protocol).

## The three pillars

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Abiotic Stress" icon="sun" href="/guides/Crop/Pineapple/abiotic/drought">
    Water deficit, waterlogging, sunburn, chilling, wind, and salinity on BRIS. Slow to moderate, block-scale, weather and drainage driven.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Nutrient Disorders" icon="flask" href="/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/potassium">
    N, K, Mg, Fe, Zn, Ca, B. Foliar sprays are the primary correction because pineapple absorbs most nutrients through the leaf axil.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Biotic Stress" icon="bug" href="/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/heart-rot">
    Diseases, insect pests, nematodes, and weeds. Individual-plant and cluster-scale. Ground confirmation essential.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Scout-first decision tree

Use what you see first to pick the pillar. The scout looks at four things on each plant, in this order.

### 1. Block-level pattern

| What you see across the block | Likely pillar |
| --- | --- |
| Whole block uniformly off, matches weather or soil map | **Abiotic** or block-wide nutrient |
| Gradient across the block following topography or drainage | **Abiotic** (waterlogging, drought) or soil-linked nutrient |
| Wet spots with collapsed plants after heavy rain | **Biotic** - [Heart rot / Phytophthora](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/heart-rot) |
| Circular expanding patches of wilted, red-yellow plants | **Biotic** - [Mealybug wilt](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/mealybug-wilt) |
| Slow diffuse decline over months, no clear pattern | **Biotic** - [Nematodes](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/nematodes) or chronic nutrient |
| Scattered dead plants along headlands, machinery tracks | **Biotic** - [Fusariosis](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/fusariosis) or mechanical injury |
| Bleached, upright leaves on sun-exposed rows | **Abiotic** - [Sunburn](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/abiotic/sunburn) |

### 2. Leaf whorl color and position (nutrient and disease decision)

Pineapple leaves are numbered by the D-leaf convention: the tallest, most-erect fully-expanded leaf is the **D-leaf** (roughly the 3rd or 4th leaf from the center) and is the reference for nutrient sampling.

| What you see | Where on the plant | Likely cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole rosette pale green | All leaves | [Nitrogen](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/nitrogen) |
| Yellow-orange older leaves, tips die back | Lower whorl | [Potassium](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/potassium) |
| Yellow between veins on older leaves, red-purple margins | Lower whorl | [Magnesium](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/magnesium) |
| Yellow young leaves, green veins | Center whorl / D-leaves | [Iron](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/iron) (chlorosis) |
| Small, pale, distorted young leaves | Center whorl | [Zinc](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/zinc) (crookneck) |
| Cracked, corky, or split young leaves and fruit | Center whorl \+ fruit | [Boron](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/boron) |
| Red-brown discoloration on leaf tips of older leaves | Lower whorl | [Calcium](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/calcium) (edge burn) or salinity |
| Yellow tips with pink flush on older leaves | Lower whorl | Early [Mealybug wilt](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/mealybug-wilt) |

### 3. Heart, base, and roots (disease decision tree)

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Center whorl leaves pull out with a foul smell | [Heart rot](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/heart-rot) (Phytophthora) |
| Whole plant tips over, black rotted base | [Root rot](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/root-rot) (Phytophthora cinnamomi) |
| Basal white rot with pink or brown stain | [Fusariosis](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/fusariosis) |
| Water-soaked black lesions on harvested fruit | [Black rot](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/black-rot) (Chalara / Thielaviopsis) |
| Waxy white or grey insects at leaf base, sticky honeydew | [Mealybugs](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/insects/mealybugs) |
| Ants trailing along the plant base | Mealybug or scale tending |
| Stunted, thick, twisted roots | [Nematodes](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/nematodes) (root-knot, reniform, or lesion) |
| White centipede-like arthropods in the soil at planting | [Symphylids](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/insects/symphylids) |

### 4. Fruit and crown (yield-critical decision tree)

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Small, misshapen, or knobby fruit | [Boron](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/boron), [Calcium](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/nutrient/calcium), or thrips at induction |
| Sunburned, bleached fruit shoulders | [Sunburn](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/abiotic/sunburn) (fruit exposed) |
| Multiple crowns or fasciated crown | Genetic / temperature at forcing |
| Silver flecking on leaves and fruit | [Thrips](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/insects/thrips) |
| Fine yellow stippling on leaves | [Mites](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/insects/mites) |
| Internal browning of ripe fruit flesh | Chilling injury during storage or field cold snap |
| Sap-oozing fruit with tunnel | Fruit borer / fruitlet core rot |
| Small tan bumps on lower leaves | [Scale](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/insects/scale) |

## Growth-phase vulnerability

Different stresses matter at different phases. Use this alongside the pattern tables.

| Phase | Months after planting | What matters most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establishment | 0 – 3 | Sucker rot, symphylids, weeds, drought at rooting |
| Early vegetative | 3 – 8 | N, weeds, mealybugs, mites, mealybug wilt onset |
| Late vegetative | 8 – 12 | K, Mg, Fe, heart rot after heavy rain, nematode decline |
| Forcing | 12 – 14 | Cold at forcing (multiple crowns), Ca and B before differentiation |
| Fruit development | 14 – 18 | Sunburn, thrips, fruit borers, mealybug wilt spread, Ca and K for fruit quality |
| Ripening | 18 – 22 | Sunburn, black rot at harvest, rodent and bird damage, sugar accumulation |

## How satellites contribute

Pineapple imagery is trickier than paddy or oil palm because of low canopy height, plastic mulch, and CAM physiology. Match the tool to the question.

| Scale | Best for | Typical alert |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Block / canopy | Slow abiotic and nutrient decline, block-wide stress | NDVI or NDRE monthly trend below phase baseline |
| Bed / cluster | Spreading disease foci (mealybug wilt, heart rot pockets) | Localized NDVI drop expanding month to month |
| Individual plant | Single dead plants, gap counting | Very-high-resolution or drone imagery |

<Warning>
  Pineapple's CAM physiology delays visible NDVI response to drought by 2 to 4 weeks. Use **NDWI** and **CWSI** rather than NDVI as the early water-stress signal. NDVI is a lagging indicator for pineapple water stress.
</Warning>

  <Step title="Read the block-level pattern">
    Uniform, gradient, wet-spot cluster, or expanding focus? Use the block-level table to pick the pillar before you walk in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Walk to suspect plants and inspect">
    Follow the [Field Walk Protocol](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/field-walk-protocol). Check whorl color, heart and base, roots, and fruit in that order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match symptoms to a specific cause">
    Use the leaf, heart, and fruit tables above to move from symptom to page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Act, record, and set a monitoring alert">
    Apply the recommended action, record the plant or block, and use the [Alerts](/guides/alerts-notifications) page to configure a follow-up so the same issue is flagged earlier next time.
  </Step>
</Steps>
