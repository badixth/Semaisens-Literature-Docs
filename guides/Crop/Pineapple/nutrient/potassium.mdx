Potassium is the single largest quality driver on pineapple. K controls fruit size, sugar accumulation (Brix), shelf life, and acid balance. Deficiency shows as **yellow-orange older leaves with tip die-back** and, at harvest, as **small, low-Brix, poor-shelf-life fruit** even if canopy looks acceptable.

**Regional priority:** High on mineral upland, BRIS, and any block older than one cycle without full K replacement.

## When pineapple is most vulnerable

From **late vegetative (month 10)** through **fruit development (month 18)**. K demand peaks between forcing and fruit filling.

## How potassium deficiency shows in satellite data

- **Block-wide NDVI below phase baseline**, especially on light soils.
- Slow, multi-month decline; not sudden.
- Less directly visible than N deficiency because K affects fruit quality more than canopy greenness.
- Best confirmed by D-leaf analysis and fruit sampling.

## Field symptoms

- **Yellow-orange chlorosis on older leaves**, starting at the tips and margins
- **Necrotic (dead) leaf tips** in advanced deficiency
- **Reduced fruit weight** and small "eyes" (fruitlets)
- **Low Brix (\< 12)** and poor flavor
- **Short shelf life** and translucent flesh at ripening
- **Increased lodging** of fruiting plants (weak peduncles)
- **Increased susceptibility** to sunburn, mealybug wilt, and heart rot

## Reduce the damage by

- **Muriate of potash (MOP, KCl)** as the standard K source for processing blocks - typically 500 to 900 kg/ha per cycle, split into 4 to 6 applications.
- **Sulphate of potash (SOP)** for fresh-market MD2 blocks - MOP chloride can depress Brix at high rates.
- **Foliar K (KNO3, K2SO4)** through the leaf axil in the 6 weeks before forcing.
- **Split applications** - single large doses leach on sandy soils.
- **Balance K with N and Mg** - typical K:Mg target 3:1 on mineral soils.
- **D-leaf analysis at 8 to 10 months** - target 3.0 to 3.5% K in the D-leaf.
- **Return crop residues** to the block at destruction to recycle K.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. K deficiency is a slow trend and quality signal, so combine an NDVI trend alert with a scheduled D-leaf and Brix check.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for potassium on pineapple:

- **NDVI trend alert** - trigger when the block's 6-week moving NDVI drops more than 5% below the phase baseline.
- **D-leaf sampling reminder** - month 8 to 10 (before forcing) and month 15 (mid fruit development).
- **Brix check** - schedule a fruit-sampling round at ripening; flag blocks with Brix below 12.
