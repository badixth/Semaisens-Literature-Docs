Iron chlorosis on pineapple is a **high-pH problem**, not a soil-supply problem. Iron is abundant in most soils but locked up above pH 6.5, particularly on coral, calcareous, and over-limed blocks. The signature is **yellow young leaves with green veins** in the center whorl.

**Regional priority:** High on coral, calcareous, and over-limed soils; common on Cayenne and Josapine grown on limestone-influenced land.

## When pineapple is most vulnerable

At all phases if soil pH is high. Most visible in the fastest-growing months (early to late vegetative) when new leaf demand for iron is highest.

## How iron deficiency shows in satellite data

- **NDRE below phase baseline** on affected zones - iron chlorosis depletes chlorophyll in young leaves directly.
- Pattern often matches known calcareous or high-pH zones (mapped soil zones or spot-lime history).
- Distinguish from N deficiency by the young-leaf pattern in the field; satellite alone cannot separate them.

## Field symptoms

- **Center whorl and young leaves pale yellow** with distinct green veins (interveinal chlorosis)
- **Older leaves remain green** (iron is immobile in the plant)
- **Small, distorted new growth** if deficiency is severe
- **Stunted plants** in chronic cases
- Symptoms disappear from newest leaves within 2 to 3 weeks of foliar chelated iron

## Reduce the damage by

- **Foliar chelated iron (Fe-EDDHA or Fe-DTPA)** for fast correction - repeat every 3 to 4 weeks until new growth is green.
- **Acidify the root zone** with ammonium sulphate rather than urea; over time, lowers pH.
- **Avoid over-liming** - do not raise pH above 6.0 on pineapple blocks.
- **Apply elemental sulfur** on high-pH blocks to gradually acidify.
- **Avoid alkaline irrigation water** or acidify it if unavoidable.
- **Do not use ferrous sulphate as a soil amendment** on high-pH soils - it re-precipitates within days. Chelates are required.
- **Soil pH test** every cycle on calcareous blocks; target pH 5.0 to 5.5.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Iron chlorosis is stable and pH-driven, so schedule pH and foliar reminders rather than event alerts.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for iron on pineapple:

- **NDRE anomaly alert** - trigger on persistent (\> 30 days) NDRE anomaly in a specific zone.
- **Soil pH reminder** - annual check on calcareous or over-limed blocks.
- **Foliar-application follow-up** - schedule a re-check 3 weeks after every chelated iron spray.
