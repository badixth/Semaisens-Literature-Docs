Mealybugs are the number-one insect pest of pineapple worldwide because they transmit **Pineapple Mealybug Wilt-associated Virus (PMWaV)**, the main viral killer of the crop. **Dysmicoccus brevipes** (pink pineapple mealybug) and **D. neobrevipes** (grey pineapple mealybug) live in the leaf axil, feed on plant sap, produce honeydew, and are protected by ants (Pheidole, Solenopsis).

**Regional priority:** Universal. Every pineapple block has some mealybugs; management is about suppression, not eradication.

## When pineapple is most vulnerable

At **all phases**. Early-vegetative outbreaks (months 3 to 6) set up late-cycle wilt losses. Fruit development (months 14 to 18) outbreaks damage fruit quality directly and open Fusarium entry sites.

## How mealybugs show in satellite data

- Not directly detectable at Sentinel scale in early stages.
- Once **mealybug wilt** develops, satellite shows expanding circular red-yellow patches (see [Mealybug wilt](/guides/Crop/Pineapple/biotic/diseases/mealybug-wilt)).
- Best detected by scheduled ground scouting, not remote sensing.

## Field symptoms

- **Waxy white or grey cottony colonies** at the leaf axil and plant base
- **Sticky honeydew** on lower leaves; may support sooty mold
- **Ants trailing** the plant base - the earliest visible signal on a walk
- **Yellowing and pink flush** on older leaves in advanced infestation (mealybug wilt onset)
- **Fruit-side colonies** on developing fruit - direct quality damage
- **Colonies at the sucker base** on planting material - the main way pests move between blocks

## Reduce the damage by

- **Clean planting material** - dip suckers in insecticide (buprofezin, thiamethoxam) or hot water (50 C, 30 min) before planting.
- **Ant control is the leverage point** - baits (hydramethylnon, fipronil) around block edges and along mulch lines break ant-mealybug tending. Without ants, natural enemies (lady beetles, parasitoid wasps) suppress mealybugs.
- **Preventive foliar insecticide** on high-risk blocks - buprofezin (IGR), spirotetramat (systemic), or thiamethoxam. Rotate modes of action to slow resistance.
- **Scout every 2 weeks** for mealybug colonies and ant trails; count colonies per plant.
- **Threshold** - action at 5 or more colonies per plant, or any expanding wilt focus.
- **Do not intercrop** with other mealybug hosts (banana, taro, ornamentals).
- **Rogue advanced plants** at wilt focus edges to slow within-block spread.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Mealybugs are ground-scouted, so alerts are scheduled walks plus MWP satellite follow-up.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for mealybugs on pineapple:

- **Bi-weekly scouting reminder** - schedule mealybug and ant walks every 2 weeks during vegetative and fruit development.
- **Ant-trail alert** - flag any block where ant trails are recorded on more than 20% of scouted plants.
- **Wilt follow-up alert** - trigger a mealybug re-inspection within 1 week on any block flagged for expanding wilt patches.
