Mites (mainly the **red spider mite Tetranychus** and **broad mite Polyphagotarsonemus latus**) build up on pineapple in hot, dry weather. Damage shows as **fine yellow stippling** on leaves, sometimes with visible webbing in severe cases. Broad mite also distorts young leaves and can damage fruit skin. Mite outbreaks are often triggered by broad-spectrum insecticide sprays that kill predator mites.

**Regional priority:** Moderate to high during dry season across all regions; higher on blocks with heavy insecticide history.

## When pineapple is most vulnerable

During prolonged **hot-dry weather** at any phase. Broad mite damage on young leaves is most damaging at early vegetative; spider mite damage on canopy leaves peaks at late vegetative and fruit development.

## How mites show in satellite data

- Not detectable at Sentinel scale in early stages.
- Advanced infestations cause block-wide slight NDVI shortfall in dry weather; hard to separate from drought.
- Ground scouting with a 10x hand lens is essential for detection.

## Field symptoms

- **Fine yellow stippling** on leaf surfaces
- **Bronzing** in advanced spider mite infestation
- **Fine webbing** on leaf undersides (spider mite)
- **Distorted, cupped young leaves** (broad mite)
- **Russeting or bronzing on fruit skin** if broad mite feeds on developing fruit
- **Confirm with hand lens** - spider mites are 0.5 mm red-orange dots; broad mites are 0.2 mm and nearly invisible without magnification

## Reduce the damage by

- **Conserve predators** - avoid broad-spectrum pyrethroids and carbamates that kill predator mites (Phytoseiulus, Amblyseius).
- **Miticide when threshold is met** - abamectin, spiromesifen, or bifenazate. Rotate modes of action.
- **Water-stress management** - well-irrigated blocks have lower mite pressure.
- **Overhead irrigation** briefly disrupts mite populations on the leaf surface.
- **Scout during dry spells** - every 2 weeks with a hand lens, focus on leaf undersides and the center whorl.
- **Threshold** - action at more than 10 spider mites per D-leaf, or any observable broad mite damage on young leaves.
- **Do not apply sulfur** in hot weather - phytotoxic on pineapple.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Mite outbreaks track hot-dry weather, so use a weather trigger plus a scheduled hand-lens scout.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for mites on pineapple:

- **Weather alert** - 14 or more consecutive days above 30 C with rainfall below 20 mm triggers a mite scout.
- **Post-insecticide alert** - schedule a mite scout 3 weeks after any broad-spectrum insecticide application.
- **Broad mite alert** - flag any block where young leaves show cupping distortion for immediate hand-lens inspection.
