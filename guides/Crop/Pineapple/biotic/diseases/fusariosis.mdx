Fusariosis, caused by **Fusarium guttiforme** (formerly F. subglutinans f. sp. ananas), is the major fungal killer of pineapple in Latin America and increasingly in Southeast Asia. It enters through wounds - mechanical injury, mealybug feeding sites, harvest tools - and causes basal rot, fruit rot, and sucker rot. Unlike Phytophthora, it does not need standing water; it needs a wound.

**Regional priority:** Very high in Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, and increasingly in Southeast Asia; moderate in Africa.

## When pineapple is most vulnerable

At all phases, but the highest incidence follows **mechanical wounding**: harvest, forcing chemical application, thinning, or mealybug outbreaks. Sucker rot appears in newly planted blocks.

## How fusariosis shows in satellite data

- **Scattered dead plants** rather than clusters - hard to see at Sentinel scale.
- **Edge and headland concentration** where machinery and harvest activity concentrate wounds.
- Confirm on the ground with basal-cut inspection (pink-brown stain, resin exudate).

## Field symptoms

- **Basal white-to-pink rot** with characteristic pink-brown stain when cut
- **Amber-colored resin exudate** on the outside of the base or stem
- **Curved, bent, or twisted fruit** with tissue collapse ("fruitlet core rot")
- **Sucker rot** on newly planted material
- **Scattered dead plants** along headlands and machinery tracks
- **Slow decline** over weeks, not sudden collapse

## Reduce the damage by

- **Use disease-free, resistant planting material** - MD2 has moderate resistance; Perola and Smooth Cayenne are highly susceptible.
- **Dip suckers and slips** in a fungicide bath (triazole or benzimidazole) before planting.
- **Avoid wounding** - handle plants with care during forcing, thinning, and harvest.
- **Control mealybugs and other wounding pests** - mealybug feeding sites are common Fusarium entry points.
- **Disinfect harvest tools** between plants on infected blocks.
- **Post-harvest fruit dip** in fungicide for export-quality fruit.
- **Destroy infected plants** promptly; do not leave rotten fruit on the block as inoculum.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Fusariosis follows wounds, so build alerts around post-forcing and post-harvest inspections.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for fusariosis on pineapple:

- **Post-forcing inspection reminder** - schedule a walk-through 3 weeks after every forcing application.
- **Post-harvest inspection reminder** - schedule a headland walk 2 weeks after every harvest.
- **Mealybug follow-up alert** - trigger a fusariosis inspection 4 weeks after any block flagged for mealybug outbreak.
