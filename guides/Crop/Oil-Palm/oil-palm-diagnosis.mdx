Oil palm is a 25-year crop with individual palms yielding bunches every 10 to 14 days. Diagnosis works at three scales at once: whole block, palm cluster, and individual palm. Use this guide to move from a field observation or a satellite alert to a specific cause and a specific action.

For the standard census and inspection sequence a scout should follow on the ground, see [Field Walk Protocol](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/field-walk-protocol).

## The three pillars

  <Card title="Abiotic Stress" icon="sun" href="/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/abiotic/drought">
    Water deficit, waterlogging, peat subsidence, acid sulfate, salinity, wind, lightning, fire. Slow, block-scale, weather-driven.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Nutrient Disorders" icon="flask" href="/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/potassium">

  </Card>

## Scout-first decision tree

Use what you see first to pick the pillar. The scout looks at four things on each palm, in this order.

### 1. Block-level pattern

| What you see across the block | Likely pillar |
| --- | --- |
| Whole block uniformly off, matches weather or soil map | **Abiotic** or block-wide nutrient |
| Gradient across the block following topography | **Abiotic** (drainage) or soil-linked nutrient |
| Scattered individual palms declining, no clear pattern | **Biotic** - trunk disease (Ganoderma) |
| Spreading focus of dying palms, expanding month to month | **Biotic** - contagious disease (Bud Rot, Fusarium, Red Ring) |
| Fronds chewed, defoliated, or skeletonized | **Biotic** - insects (bagworms, nettle caterpillars) |
| Spears broken, bored, or rotten | **Biotic** - Rhino beetle, palm weevil, or Bud Rot |

### 2. Frond color and position (nutrient decision tree)

Frond color patterns in oil palm are so specific they are often named after the deficiency. Match what you see to the table.

| What you see | Where on the palm | Likely cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall pale green, whole canopy | All fronds | [Nitrogen](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/nitrogen) |
| Confluent orange spots, older fronds | Mid to lower canopy | [Potassium](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/potassium) (mineral soils) |
| Bright orange whole fronds, older first | Mid to lower canopy | [Magnesium](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/magnesium) (light and sandy soils) |
| Hooked, fish-tail, or little-leaf fronds | Young spear fronds | [Boron](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/boron) |
| Mid-crown yellow-green fronds | Mid canopy | [Copper](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/copper) (peat and sandy soils) |
| Chlorotic youngest fronds | Spear and just below | [Iron](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/iron) (high-pH soils) |
| Older fronds bronze or purple | Lower canopy | [Phosphorus](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/nutrient/phosphorus) |

### 3. Trunk (disease decision tree)

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Bracket fungus at base \+ one-sided frond collapse | [Ganoderma / Basal Stem Rot](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/biotic/diseases/ganoderma) |
| Bracket fungus mid-trunk | [Upper Stem Rot](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/biotic/diseases/upper-stem-rot) |
| Wilting spreads outward from one palm, no brackets | Fusarium Wilt or Bud Rot |
| Spear rot with necrotic center | Bud Rot |
| Ring of red-brown tissue when trunk is sliced | Red Ring |
| Bore holes with frass at trunk | Palm Weevil |

### 4. Spear and youngest fronds (growing-point decision tree)

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Spear pulls out easily, rotten inside | Bud rot, or late-stage rhino beetle |
| V-shaped notches in unopened frond | Rhino beetle feeding |
| Hooked, deformed young frond | Boron deficiency |
| Chlorotic young frond | Iron deficiency or Crown Disease |

## Stand-age vulnerability

Different stresses matter at different ages. Use this alongside the pattern tables.

| Stand age | What matters most |
| --- | --- |
| Nursery (\< 12 months) | Cercospora, anthracnose, nursery blast, boron, iron, rats |
| Immature (1 to 3 years) | Rhino beetle, rats, Imperata, boron, drought, waterlogging |
| Yield build-up (3 to 8 years) | Potassium, magnesium, drought, Ganoderma (early), bagworms, nettle caterpillars |
| Peak yield (8 to 18 years) | Potassium, magnesium, Ganoderma, drought lag effects, bunch pests |
| Decline (18\+ years) | Ganoderma cluster expansion, replanting decisions |

## How satellites contribute

Oil palm imagery works at three scales at once. Match the tool to the question.

| Scale | Best for | Typical alert |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Block / canopy | Slow abiotic and nutrient decline, block-wide stress | NDVI or EVI monthly trend below stand-age baseline |
| Palm cluster | Spreading disease foci (Ganoderma, Bud Rot) | Localized zonal NDVI drop expanding month to month |
| Individual palm | Single declining or dead palms | Very-high-resolution or drone imagery; crown count anomalies |

<Warning>
  Oil palm change is slow. A weekly NDVI review is over-monitoring for most abiotic and nutrient issues. Monthly is usually enough, except during outbreak or replanting phases.
</Warning>

## Decision flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the block-level pattern">
    Uniform, gradient, scattered, or spreading? Use the block-level table to pick the pillar before you walk in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Walk to suspect palms and inspect">
    Follow the [Field Walk Protocol](/guides/Crop/Oil-Palm/field-walk-protocol). Check crown, frond color and position, trunk, and spear in that order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match symptoms to a specific cause">
    Use the frond, trunk, and spear tables above to move from symptom to page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Act, record, and set a monitoring alert">
    Apply the recommended action, record the palm or block, and use the [Alerts](/guides/alerts-notifications) page to configure a follow-up so the same issue is flagged earlier next time.
  </Step>
</Steps>
