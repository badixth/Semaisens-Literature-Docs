Wind and lightning are the two episodic weather events that produce sudden, visible damage in oil palm. Wind damage is progressive with palm height (taller palms suffer more), while lightning damage strikes randomly but clusters around tall palms and metal structures. Both create wounds that become entry points for palm weevil, rhinoceros beetle, and stem-rot diseases, so the follow-up sanitation matters as much as the initial damage assessment.

**Regional priority:** High in cyclone-prone zones (Philippines, Indonesia storm belt, Pacific coast of Central America, northeastern Madagascar) and lightning-prone equatorial regions. Continuously important on tall old palms nearing replanting.

## When oil palm is most vulnerable

Wind: peak yield through decline (8\+ years) when palms are tallest. Lightning: any age, but tall mature palms are struck most often.

## How wind and lightning damage show in satellite data

- **Sudden NDVI drop** across affected blocks after a storm event - one of the fastest satellite-detectable changes in oil palm.
- **High-resolution imagery** shows the pattern - wind damage is directional (streaks aligned with wind direction), lightning damage is scattered point clusters.
- **True-color imagery** shows broken fronds, snapped crowns, and dead palms clearly within days of the event.
- **Historical weather event correlation** - flag any block that shows NDVI drop within a week of a recorded storm.

<Warning>
  Damaged palms with broken fronds and open wounds become weevil and stem-rot targets within weeks. Post-storm sanitation is as important as the initial damage response.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

**Wind damage:**

- **Broken fronds** hanging from the crown or on the ground.
- **Snapped or bent crowns** on tall palms.
- **Whole palm uprooted** in severe cyclones, especially on peat or shallow-rooted soils.
- **Directional pattern** - damage aligns with wind direction across the block.

**Lightning damage:**

- **Clusters of 5 to 15 dead palms** around a central strike point, sometimes with a slight scorch pattern.
- **Sudden death within days** of the strike, without prior disease symptoms.
- **Charred bark or spear** at the strike point palm.
- **Adjacent palms decline over weeks** as root systems are damaged by the ground current.
- **Scattered lightning-strike clusters** across a plantation follow storm-cell paths.

## Reduce the damage by

**Wind damage response:**

- **Post-event census** - inspect every block within a week of a significant wind event.
- **Prune broken fronds cleanly** - do not leave shattered petioles hanging; they are weevil bait.
- **Uproot and salvage-fell** heavily damaged palms rather than leaving them.
- **Wound sealing** on major trunk wounds with wound paint or copper-based dressing.
- **Improve root anchoring** in high-wind zones - deeper mound planting, cover crops, and controlled water tables improve wind resistance on peat.
- **Consider windbreaks** at plantation edges in cyclone-prone regions.

**Lightning damage response:**

- **Post-event census** after major thunderstorm activity.
- **Fell and destroy** confirmed lightning-strike palms and their obviously affected neighbors within the cluster.
- **Monitor adjacent palms** for delayed decline over the following weeks.
- **Lightning-protection systems** on premium estates - lightning rods installed on the tallest palms of a block, grounded well below the root zone. Economically justified only for high-value blocks in high-lightning regions.
- **Do not replant into the strike zone** immediately - the ground current may have damaged soil biology and adjacent root systems.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Wind and lightning alerts pair storm-detection with rapid imagery review - the imagery confirms and locates the damage the weather triggered.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for wind and lightning damage on oil palm:

- **Weather alert** - wind gust or lightning-strike density crossing thresholds triggers a post-event inspection reminder.
- **NDVI drop alert** within 7 days of the storm event.
- **New Imagery alert** during and immediately after monsoon and cyclone seasons.
- **Sanitation follow-up reminder** - schedule a wound-management pass 2 to 4 weeks after any major event.
