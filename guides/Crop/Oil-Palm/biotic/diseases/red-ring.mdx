Red Ring disease is caused by the nematode _Bursaphelenchus cocophilus_ and is transmitted primarily by the American palm weevil (_Rhynchophorus palmarum_). The disease name comes from the characteristic red-brown ring visible when the trunk of an infected palm is cut in cross-section. It kills palms within months once symptoms appear and is a serious problem across the American tropics.

**Regional priority:** High in Latin America (Colombia, Ecuador, Brazil, Venezuela, Central America). Not present in Southeast Asia or Africa.

## When oil palm is most vulnerable

Any age from immature onward. Palms in the 3 to 15 year range are most commonly affected. Recent planting near infected coconut, ornamental palm, or oil palm sites is a strong risk factor.

## How Red Ring shows in satellite data

- **Individual-palm decline** in high-resolution imagery, progressing from crown yellowing to full collapse over 2 to 4 months.
- **Scattered pattern** across the plantation rather than clustered - vector-driven distribution follows weevil movement.
- Not distinguishable from other systemic diseases (Bud Rot, Fusarium Wilt) in imagery. Ground diagnosis is required.
- **Weevil trap catch data** paired with imagery gives a much stronger picture than either alone.

<Warning>
  Red Ring is confirmed only by cutting the trunk. Do not misdiagnose from crown symptoms alone - Bud Rot, Fusarium Wilt, and Red Ring all present with crown decline.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- **Progressive frond yellowing** starting with the mid-canopy fronds and progressing up and down.
- **Shortened and stunted new fronds** emerging from the crown.
- **Reduced bunch production** in the months leading to full decline.
- **Death within 2 to 4 months** of first visible symptoms.
- **Red-brown ring** 3 to 5 cm inside the trunk, visible in cross-section - the diagnostic sign.
- **Palm weevil bore holes** and frass near the trunk base.

## Reduce the damage by

- **Trap and remove palm weevils** - pheromone-baited traps combined with plant volatiles significantly reduce vector populations. Trap density and maintenance are the key controls.
- **Sanitation felling** - fell and destroy infected palms as soon as diagnosis is confirmed. Do not leave infected trunk material on site - it becomes a weevil breeding site.
- **Destroy weevil breeding sites** - remove decaying palm material, fallen trunks, and pruning debris from the block.
- **Do not prune excessively** or leave large wounds - weevils are attracted to fresh wounds and volatiles.
- **Coordinated regional weevil management** - one estate cannot control weevil populations alone if neighbors leave breeding sites intact.
- **Screen planting material** for weevil damage and possible nematode carriage.
- **No effective chemical treatment** for infected palms - focus resources on vector control.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Red Ring alerts should combine weevil trap data with imagery review, not imagery alone.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for Red Ring on oil palm:

- **Weekly weevil trap review** in endemic zones.
- **Palm-cluster NDVI drop alert** - trigger on scattered individual palm decline (not clustered - use pattern to distinguish from Ganoderma).
- **Sanitation compliance reminder** - schedule regular verification that felled palms have been destroyed and breeding sites cleared.
