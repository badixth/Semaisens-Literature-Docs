Bud Rot, known regionally as **Pudrición del Cogollo (PC)**, is the most devastating oil palm disease in Latin America. Once the growing point is destroyed, the palm cannot recover. Whole plantations have been abandoned in Colombia, Ecuador, and Panama after epidemics. The dominant causal agent is _Phytophthora palmivora_, though the disease complex involves multiple secondary pathogens.

**Regional priority:** Extremely high in Latin America (Colombia, Ecuador, Panama, Costa Rica, Peru, Brazil). Present but less devastating in West Africa. Rare in Southeast Asia.

## When oil palm is most vulnerable

All ages, but young palms (3 to 8 years) are most susceptible and die fastest. Wet, humid, high-rainfall conditions strongly favor the disease. Poorly drained lowland plantations and blocks planted on former forest are highest risk.

## How Bud Rot shows in satellite data

- **Spreading focus of dying palms** - the classic epidemiological pattern. A single infected palm becomes a cluster, then a patch, then a block.
- **Individual-palm decline** visible in high-resolution or drone imagery: the spear turns brown and collapses, then the whole crown yellows and falls.
- **Block-scale NDVI decline** on affected areas over months as mortality accumulates.
- **True-color imagery** can show the characteristic pale-brown spear against the surrounding green canopy.

<Warning>
  Bud Rot spreads faster than most oil palm diseases. A weekly imagery review is justified in outbreak zones. Coordinated regional monitoring is more effective than farm-by-farm response.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

Early stage:

- **Spear rot** - the youngest unopened frond turns yellow-brown, becomes soft, and can be pulled out easily.
- **Foul odor** from the growing point when the spear is removed.
- **Necrotic tissue** at the base of the spear extending into the bud.

Mid stage:

- **Progressive yellowing** and death of surrounding young fronds.
- **Chlorosis** spreading from the center of the crown outward.
- **New fronds fail to emerge** or emerge deformed and small.

Late stage:

- **Whole crown collapse** - the top of the palm falls away.
- Trunk may remain standing for months but is functionally dead.
- **Clustered mortality** in the block, spreading outward from initial focus.

## Reduce the damage by

- **Early detection is everything** - weekly to biweekly inspection of every palm in outbreak zones. Removing the diseased tissue at the earliest spear-rot stage can sometimes save the palm.
- **Sanitation surgery** - remove all necrotic tissue from the bud, disinfect with a copper-based fungicide, and cover the exposed area. Only effective at very early stages.
- **Eradication of late-stage palms** - fell and destroy palms past the sanitation threshold. Do not leave infected material on site.
- **Plant resistant material** - **interspecific OxG hybrids** (_Elaeis oleifera_ x _E. guineensis_) show strong Bud Rot tolerance and are now the standard replant material in high-risk Latin American zones.
- **Improve drainage** - Bud Rot is a wet-season disease. Standing water and poor drainage strongly increase incidence.
- **Manage the vector complex** - insect vectors (especially _Rhynchophorus_ weevils and some leafhoppers) contribute to disease spread. Weevil trapping and vector management are part of an integrated response.
- **Coordinate regionally** - Bud Rot ignores farm boundaries. Regional monitoring committees and coordinated felling programs have been the successful model in Colombia.
- **Do not replant susceptible material** in outbreak zones - use tolerant OxG hybrids even at higher planting cost.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. In outbreak zones, Bud Rot deserves the highest alerting priority - weekly imagery, weekly ground inspection, and immediate action on any spear-rot palm.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for Bud Rot on oil palm:

- **Weekly imagery review** in outbreak zones - high-resolution or drone.
- **Palm-cluster NDVI drop alert** - trigger on a drop of 0.10 or more in any 3-palm cluster.
- **Weather alert** - sustained rainfall and high humidity events preceding scouting reminders.
- **Regional coordination reminder** - align inspection cycles with neighboring farms and any active regional monitoring program.
