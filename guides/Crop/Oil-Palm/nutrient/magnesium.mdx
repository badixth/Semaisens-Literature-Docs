Magnesium is central to chlorophyll production, and oil palm shows deficiency more dramatically than almost any other crop. The classic signature is **"orange frond"** - whole older fronds turning a startling bright orange while younger fronds remain green. It is most common on light, sandy, or heavily leached soils, and is induced by excess K application without matching Mg.

**Regional priority:** Universal on light and sandy soils; commonly seen alongside K deficiency on the same blocks. On peat, Mg deficiency is much rarer than Cu, N, and K.

## When oil palm is most vulnerable

From yield build-up (3 years) onward. Mg demand rises with bunch export, and chronic deficiency accumulates over multiple seasons on light soils.

## How magnesium deficiency shows in satellite data

- **Distinctive orange tint** visible in true-color imagery on affected blocks - one of the few nutrient disorders detectable in RGB imagery in oil palm.
- **Block-wide NDVI decline** on chronic sites, similar in magnitude to K deficiency.
- Slow trend rather than sudden drop; monthly to quarterly review is appropriate.
- Patchy signatures within a block often follow soil texture (worse on sandy patches).

<Note>
  Mg orange-frond and K orange-spotting can look similar from above. The distinguishing signal is on the ground: Mg turns _whole fronds_ orange; K produces _spots_ on the leaflets that eventually merge. Both hit older fronds first.
</Note>

## Field symptoms

- **Whole older fronds turn bright orange** - dramatic and unmistakable when severe.
- Orange color starts on the sunlight-exposed side of the frond and spreads inward.
- **Older fronds affected first**, younger fronds remain green.
- Green midrib may remain visible through the orange leaflets in early stages.
- Reduced frond dry weight and shorter fronds in chronic cases.
- Reduced bunch weight and stunted growth on immature palms.

## Reduce the damage by

- **Apply kieserite (magnesium sulphate)** as the standard Mg source - typically 1 to 2 kg per palm per year, split into 2 doses.
- **Apply dolomite** on acid soils - provides both Mg and lime, correcting pH at the same time.
- **Match K:Mg ratio** - typical target ratio is roughly 2:1 (K:Mg) by weight. Excessive K without matching Mg is a common cause of induced Mg deficiency.
- **Use magnesium-containing compound fertilizers** where custom mixing is not available.
- **Empty fruit bunch (EFB) mulching** contributes modest Mg along with K.
- **Correct soil acidity** on strongly acid soils - Mg availability drops sharply below pH 4.5.
- **Avoid over-liming on peat** - excess Ca and Mg on peat can lock up other micronutrients.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Mg deficiency tracks with K management and light-soil zones; combine soil map with imagery review.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for magnesium on oil palm:

- **True-color imagery review** - schedule visual inspection twice per year on light-soil blocks; the orange tint often shows before NDVI moves substantially.
- **Annual leaf sampling reminder** for frond 17 K and Mg together.
- **Soil-zone map overlay** - flag sandy or light-soil zones for higher Mg allocation in the annual fertilizer plan.
