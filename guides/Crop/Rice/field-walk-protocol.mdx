Rice scouting is not the same as oil palm scouting. You are looking at a uniform seasonal canopy of many small plants, not a stand of long-lived individuals. A rice field changes in days, not months. Use this protocol as the checklist for a routine weekly walk, a follow-up on a satellite alert, or a targeted inspection during a critical growth stage.

## When to run this protocol

- **Weekly** during active growth (tillering through grain fill)
- **Twice weekly** during booting to heading (highest yield risk from blast, BPH, stem borer)
- **Immediately** on any field flagged by a satellite alert or when neighboring fields report an outbreak
- **Within 48 hours** of a weather event (heavy rain, storm, prolonged cloudy or humid spell, cold snap)
- **Before and after** every fertilizer, herbicide, or pesticide application

## What to bring

- Field map with block or plot IDs and the planned walk transect
- Field record sheet or mobile app tied to the field data model
- Leaf color chart (LCC) or SPAD meter (N status)
- White tray or beat sheet (BPH, planthopper, egg mass sampling)
- Sweep net (leafhoppers, adult moths, rice bug)
- Small hand lens (10x) for lesions and hopper nymphs
- Sample bags for leaf, tiller, panicle, and soil samples
- Camera or phone (photograph every anomaly)
- GPS for stop-level records
- Rubber boots and a machete

## Choose a walk pattern

Unlike oil palm, you cannot inspect every plant. Sample the field along a fixed pattern so results are repeatable week to week.

| Field shape and size | Recommended pattern | Stops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small (\< 0.5 ha), regular | Diagonal (single X) | 5 stops (four corners \+ center) |
| Medium (0.5 to 2 ha), regular | Double diagonal (X) | 8 to 10 stops |
| Large (\> 2 ha) or irregular | W-pattern or zig-zag | 10 to 15 stops |
| Following a satellite alert | Straight line through the flagged zone \+ one control stop outside | 5 to 8 stops in-zone, 2 outside |

At each stop, inspect a **1 m x 1 m quadrat** (or 20 hills for transplanted paddy). Always keep the same stop count and quadrat size on the same field so the trend is comparable.

<Tip>
  Walk into the wind where possible. Insects fly and jump away from you rather than into your face, and you disturb fewer nymphs before you can count them.
</Tip>

## The 5-step inspection sequence

Run each step at every stop, in this order. Skipping a step is the most common cause of missed diagnoses.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Stand and canopy from above">
    Look at the stop from standing height first. This tells you about whole-field uniformity and recent (days to weeks) stress before you get close.

    - **Canopy color** - uniform green, pale, dark, patchy, or yellowing? (N, S, drought, salinity)
    - **Canopy height** - even, or shorter in patches? (Waterlogging, poor soil, tungro, zinc)
    - **Tiller density** - dense, thin, or gaps? (Poor establishment, gall midge, stem borer, weeds)
    - **Standing water** - present, uneven, drained, or scummy? (Irrigation status, iron toxicity, algal bloom)
    - **Weed pressure** - grasses, sedges, or broadleaf mixed in with the crop? (Weed competition)
    - **Lodging** - plants leaning or flattened in patches? (K deficiency, storm, sheath blight, BPH hopperburn)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Leaf color and position (nutrient and disease decision)">
    Bend down and pick 5 to 10 representative hills. Note _which_ leaves are affected and _where_ on the plant.

    - **Whole canopy pale, older leaves first** - [Nitrogen](/guides/Crop/Rice/nutrient/nitrogen)
    - **Whole canopy pale, young leaves first** - [Sulfur](/guides/Crop/Rice/nutrient/sulfur) (opposite of N)
    - **Yellow-brown scorching along older leaf tips and margins** - [Potassium](/guides/Crop/Rice/nutrient/potassium)
    - **Dark green, stunted, reddish-purple older leaves** - [Phosphorus](/guides/Crop/Rice/nutrient/phosphorus)
    - **Patchy dusty-brown blotches on lower leaves at early tillering** - [Zinc](/guides/Crop/Rice/nutrient/zinc) (high-pH soils)
    - **Bronze-orange stippling on lower leaves in wet, acid zones** - [Iron toxicity](/guides/Crop/Rice/nutrient/iron-toxicity)
    - **Diamond-shaped lesions, gray center and brown border** - [Blast](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/blast) (leaf)
    - **Yellow wavy lesions from the leaf tip, bacterial ooze in morning** - [Bacterial leaf blight](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/bacterial-leaf-blight)
    - **Folded leaves with white longitudinal streaks** - [Leaffolder](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/insects/leaffolder)
    - **Yellow-orange stunted plants, few tillers** - [Tungro](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/tungro)

    Take the LCC or SPAD reading here. Record the reading, not just "pale" or "green".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tiller base, sheath, and stem">
    Part the canopy and inspect the base of 5 to 10 hills. This is where most disease and insect activity starts, hidden from above.

    - **Oval greenish-grey lesions on lower sheaths, spreading up** - [Sheath blight](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/sheath-blight)
    - **Discolored, rotting flag-leaf sheath, panicle stuck inside** - [Sheath rot](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/sheath-rot)
    - **Central deadheart (dead central shoot) that pulls out easily** - [Stem borer](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/insects/stem-borers) (vegetative stage)
    - **Silver-shoot (onion-leaf) tiller with no panicle** - [Gall midge](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/insects/gall-midge)
    - **Sticky honeydew, sooty mold, hoppers at the base** - [Brown planthopper](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/insects/brown-planthopper)
    - **Abnormally tall, pale, thin seedlings** - [Bakanae](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/bakanae) (seedling/early)
    - **Frass, bore holes, or split stems** - Stem borer (later instars)

    Tap 5 hills over a white tray. Count hoppers, spiders, and other insects. Record the count per hill.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Panicle and grain (only from booting onward)">
    Once panicles are exerted, check panicle load and any damage. This is the yield-critical inspection.

    - **Whiteheads (empty, upright, pale panicles)** - [Neck blast](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/blast), [stem borer](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/insects/stem-borers), or terminal drought
    - **Brown to black rotted collar just below the panicle** - Neck blast
    - **Yellow-green fungal balls replacing grains** - [False smut](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/diseases/false-smut)
    - **Sucked, discolored, empty grains at milk stage \+ strong bug odor** - [Rice bug](/guides/Crop/Rice/biotic/insects/rice-bug)
    - **Partial panicle emergence, discolored sheath** - Sheath rot
    - **Panicles hanging, dry, or shattering** - Bird damage, late-stage blast, or over-mature
    - **Grain fill uneven** - Cold, heat, or nutrient stress at flowering

    Count filled vs unfilled grains on 5 panicles. Record the percentage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Water, soil, and field edge">
    Finish the stop by looking at the ground and the bund.

    - **Water depth** - too deep, too shallow, or absent? (Submergence, drought, weed pressure)
    - **Water color and film** - rust film, oily sheen (iron toxicity), green algal bloom (excess N), clear (normal)
    - **Bund integrity** - cracks, leaks, weed hosts on the bund? (Rat holes, weed seed source)
    - **Rat runs and cuts** - chewed tillers or panicles near the bund?
    - **Snail and shell counts** - golden apple snail damage on young transplants?
    - **Herbicide edge burn** - yellowing or bleaching in a straight line? (Drift or misapplication)

    Photograph any anomaly. Record the GPS point of the stop.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to record per stop

Minimum record at every stop, whether the field is normal or not:

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Field and stop ID | GPS if available |
| Date and time | Inspection date and time of day |
| Growth stage | Seedling / tillering / booting / heading / grain fill / maturity — **what you see, not what the band says.** These six map onto the five band phases at [Rice Diagnosis](/guides/Crop/Rice/rice-abiotic-stress#growth-stage-vulnerability); the mapping is not one to one. |
| Days after establishment | Days from sowing or transplanting |
| Canopy note | Uniform / patchy / lodged / etc. |
| Leaf color note | LCC or SPAD reading \+ which leaves affected |
| Tiller base note | Clean / lesions / deadhearts / hoppers per hill |
| Panicle note (if applicable) | Whiteheads / smut balls / % filled grains |
| Water and soil note | Depth, color, film |
| Insect counts | Per hill or per sweep, by species where possible |
| Suspected cause | Diagnosis from the [Rice Diagnosis](/guides/Crop/Rice/rice-abiotic-stress) tables |
| Action taken | Sample collected, treatment planned, marked for follow-up |
| Follow-up date | When to re-inspect |

## Field-level summary to record

After walking the field, record:

- Number of stops inspected
- Number and location of abnormal stops
- Pattern (scattered, edge, gradient with topography, whole-field, spreading focus)
- Average LCC or SPAD reading across all stops
- Recent weather, water management, or input events
- Growth stage and days-after-establishment at the field level
- Recommendation (treat, resample, escalate, or continue monitoring)

## Pattern reading (what it likely means)

Use the walk-wide pattern to decide which pillar to open in the [Rice Diagnosis](/guides/Crop/Rice/rice-abiotic-stress) guide.

| Pattern across your stops | Likely pillar |
| --- | --- |
| Whole field uniformly off, matches weather or soil map | **Abiotic** or field-wide nutrient |
| Gradient across the field following topography or water flow | **Abiotic** (drainage, salinity) or soil-linked nutrient |
| Circular expanding pale or dry patches | **Biotic** - hopperburn (BPH) or sheath blight focus |
| Straight-line edge burn | Herbicide drift or bund-line salinity |
| Scattered individual hills stunted or yellow | **Biotic** - tungro, gall midge, or virus |
| Sudden defoliation between two walks | **Biotic** - armyworm or hail |
| Sudden canopy collapse after rain | **Abiotic** - submergence, or **biotic** - BLB after storm |
| Whiteheads at heading, no lesions | Stem borer or terminal drought |
| Whiteheads at heading, neck lesions | Neck blast |

## Linking to satellite alerts

If you were sent to this field by a satellite alert:

1. Confirm the alerted zone on the ground. Walk into it and take at least 3 in-zone stops \+ 2 control stops outside the zone.
2. Record whether the pattern matches (whole-field, gradient, cluster, edge).
3. Cross-check the index that moved (NDVI, NDRE, NDWI) against the [Rice Diagnosis](/guides/Crop/Rice/rice-abiotic-stress) decision flow.
4. Use the [Rice Diagnosis](/guides/Crop/Rice/rice-abiotic-stress) tables to move from the field pattern to a specific cause.
5. Set a follow-up alert on the same field or zone via the [Alerts](/guides/alerts-notifications) page.

## Safety and hygiene

- Wash boots between fields to avoid moving blast spores, BLB, sheath blight sclerotia, and weed seeds.
- Do not enter another farm's field without permission, even to sample.
- Watch for snakes in the canopy, especially at heading.
- If you touch nettle-like plants or suspect pesticide residue, do not touch your face; wash hands before eating.
