Sulfur deficiency has become more common as farmers moved from ammonium sulphate to sulfur-free urea, and from single super phosphate to sulfur-free DAP. It looks very similar to N deficiency but appears on the _younger_ leaves first because S is not mobile in the plant.

## When rice is most vulnerable

From tillering through panicle initiation. S is needed for protein synthesis, so a shortage limits tillering, chlorophyll, and grain quality.

## How sulfur deficiency shows in satellite data

- **Low NDVI and NDRE** similar to N deficiency, but the pattern is often **not corrected by N top-dressing** - a diagnostic sign in itself.
- Uniform across the field if the whole field uses S-free fertilizer; patchy where S has been variably applied.
- Confirm by contrasting with a small N-only test strip - if the strip does not green up, suspect S.

## Field symptoms

- Pale-yellow to yellow-green **young** leaves (compare to N deficiency, where the _older_ leaves yellow first)
- Uniform yellowing across the whole leaf blade, not scorched edges
- Reduced tillering and stunted growth
- Delayed heading and reduced grain filling

## Reduce the damage by

- **Switch to a sulfur-containing fertilizer** - ammonium sulphate, single super phosphate, or sulphate of potash instead of the S-free equivalents.
- **Apply gypsum** (calcium sulphate) on chronically S-deficient soils - typically 100 to 200 kg per hectare.
- **Return crop residues and organic amendments** - straw and manure contribute meaningful S.
- **Do not diagnose from color alone** - N and S deficiency look similar; use an N test strip or leaf analysis to confirm.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Go to the [Alerts](/guides/alerts-notifications) page and configure a rule so a field that stays pale despite adequate N is flagged for a sulfur check.

Recommended settings for sulfur on rice:

- **NDRE threshold alert** during tillering.
- **Manual review flag**: if a field triggers a low-NDRE alert and the recent management log shows N top-dressing was already applied, suspect S (or Zn) rather than N.
