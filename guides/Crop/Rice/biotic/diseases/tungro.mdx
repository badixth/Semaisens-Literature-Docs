Rice tungro disease is caused by two viruses (RTBV and RTSV) transmitted by the green leafhopper (_Nephotettix virescens_) and related species. It is the most damaging viral disease of rice in South and Southeast Asia. There is no cure once infection is established; management is entirely preventive.

## When rice is most vulnerable

Early - from seedling through active tillering. Late infection causes far less yield loss than infection within the first 30 days.

## How tungro shows in satellite data

- **Yellow-orange patches with reduced NDVI** appearing in the first 30 to 60 days.
- Patches are typically **irregular and spreading** as leafhoppers move outward from a focus.
- Field edges bordering leafhopper reservoirs (grasses, older rice fields nearby) often affected first.
- Signature overlaps with iron toxicity and N deficiency; confirm with leafhopper trapping and symptom check.

<Warning>
  Tungro looks like a nutrient problem from above. If a fertilizer top-dressing does not green up the affected patches within a week, suspect tungro or iron toxicity.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- Yellow to orange-yellow discoloration, starting on the tip of young leaves and progressing downward
- Stunted plants with reduced tillering
- Delayed flowering, sterile or partially filled panicles
- Symptoms appear 1 to 2 weeks after leafhopper feeding

## Reduce the damage by

- **Grow resistant varieties** where tungro is chronic (IR64 and several IRRI lines carry useful resistance).
- **Synchronized planting** across a whole area breaks the leafhopper build-up cycle - avoid staggered planting.
- **Destroy volunteer rice and grass hosts** between seasons.
- **Manage the vector** - inspect early for green leafhopper; apply a selective insecticide only if scouting justifies it. Broad-spectrum insecticides can trigger BPH outbreaks and are counter-productive.
- **Do not replant** heavily infected fields with a susceptible variety - the source of inoculum remains high.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Configure early-establishment alerts so any patchy yellowing within the first 30 days is flagged for scouting.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for tungro on rice:

- **NDVI threshold alert** at 21 to 45 days after transplanting.
- **New Imagery alert** during the vector active period so patchy yellowing is caught within days.
