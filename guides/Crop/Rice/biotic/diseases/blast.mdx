Rice blast, caused by _Pyricularia oryzae_, is the most damaging disease of rice worldwide. It attacks the leaf, collar, node, neck, and panicle. Neck blast is the most destructive form because a rotted neck causes complete grain loss on that panicle.

## When rice is most vulnerable

Leaf blast: from seedling through tillering. Neck and panicle blast: from booting through heading. Cool, humid, cloudy weather with heavy dew or fog strongly favors epidemics.

## How rice blast shows in satellite data

- **Rapid NDVI drop** across zones with dense canopy and heavy N application, especially after several days of cool, humid weather.
- **Whiteheads at heading** show as pale patches in high-resolution imagery, similar to drought whiteheads - separate them with weather history (blast follows humidity; drought follows dryness).
- Pattern is often **field-scale during epidemics** and does not respect topography (unlike abiotic stress).

<Warning>
  Satellites narrow _where_ to scout but cannot confirm the disease. Blast, brown spot, and abiotic stress can look similar in imagery. Always confirm with a scout before spraying.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- **Leaf blast**: diamond-shaped or spindle-shaped lesions with gray centers and brown borders on leaves
- **Collar blast**: brown lesion where the leaf blade meets the sheath
- **Node blast**: blackened, rotted node; stem may break at the node
- **Neck blast**: brown to black rotted collar just below the panicle, causing the panicle to hang, dry out, and produce whiteheads
- **Panicle blast**: lesions on panicle branches; poor grain fill

## Reduce the damage by

- **Grow resistant varieties** - the primary control; rotate resistance genes across seasons where possible.
- **Avoid excessive N** and split N applications - lush high-N canopies are highly susceptible.
- **Balance N with K, Si (silicon), and appropriate P.**
- **Improve field drainage** and avoid continuous deep flooding on susceptible fields.
- **Apply fungicide** (tricyclazole or isoprothiolane) preventively at booting on susceptible varieties in high-risk environments - not curatively after neck blast is visible.
- **Destroy stubble and volunteer rice** to break the disease cycle.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Combine a weather-based blast risk alert with an NDVI drop alert to flag susceptible fields before neck blast can develop.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for blast on rice:

- **Weather alert** - cool nights (below 22 C) and high humidity (above 90%) sustained for 3\+ nights, especially at booting.
- **NDVI drop alert** - trigger on a drop of 0.10 or more during booting to heading on susceptible varieties.
