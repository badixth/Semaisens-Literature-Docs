Grass weeds - especially barnyardgrass (_Echinochloa crus-galli_ and _E. colona_) - are the most damaging weed group in rice. They mimic rice at early stages, compete for the same nutrients and light, and can cut yields by 50% or more when uncontrolled. Direct-seeded rice is particularly vulnerable.

## When rice is most vulnerable

The first 30 to 45 days after emergence or transplanting - the critical weed-free period. Weeds emerging after that window cause less yield loss.

## How grass weeds show in satellite data

- **Uniformly high NDVI** on weedy fields at establishment that masks the underlying poor rice stand - a false-positive risk.
- **Uneven, tall canopy roughness** later in the season as weeds emerge above the rice canopy.
- **Weed panicles emerging above rice** create visible pale flecks in high-resolution imagery at heading.
- Compare the field's NDVI trajectory to the [ecosystem baseline](/guides/Crop/Rice/rice-ecosystems) - a field that reaches high NDVI too early likely has weed competition.

<Warning>
  Weed detection is the weakest area for satellite imagery in rice. NDVI does not distinguish weed biomass from rice biomass. Ground scouting in the first 30 days is essential.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- Grass seedlings that look nearly identical to young rice - hardest to spot early
- Barnyardgrass: rounded, hairless leaf collar; no ligule (rice has a ligule)
- Weeds emerging in gaps between rice hills
- Later: weed heads taller than the rice, distinctive seed heads

## Reduce the damage by

- **Use clean, weed-free seed** - contaminated seed is a major source of weed infestation.
- **Land preparation** - thorough puddling reduces weed emergence in transplanted rice.
- **Maintain 3 to 5 cm standing water** for the first 30 days in irrigated systems to suppress grass weed germination.
- **Timely transplanting** to give rice a head start on weeds.
- **Hand or mechanical weeding** at 15 and 30 days after transplanting on small holdings.
- **Pre-emergence herbicide** (pretilachlor or oxadiargyl) within 3 to 5 days of transplanting. **Butachlor is banned in Malaysia** - see [Pesticide registration](/snippets/pesticide-registration).
- **Post-emergence herbicide** (bispyribac-sodium, penoxsulam, or cyhalofop) at 2 to 4 leaf stage of weeds if pre-emergence was skipped or has broken.
- **Rotate herbicide modes of action** to slow resistance development in Echinochloa.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Focus alerts on the first 30 days when weed control still recovers yield.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for grass weeds on rice:

- **NDVI trajectory alert** at 15 to 30 days after transplanting - trigger if NDVI is well above the expected baseline for that stage (weeds contributing to greenness).
- **New Imagery alert** during the critical weed-free period to prompt weekly scouting.
