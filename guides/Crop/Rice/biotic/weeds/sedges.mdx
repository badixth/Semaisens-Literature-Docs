Sedges - notably purple nutsedge (_Cyperus rotundus_), rice flatsedge (_C. iria_), and small-flower umbrella-sedge (_C. difformis_) - are the second most damaging weed group in rice after grasses. They compete strongly for nutrients and, in the case of _C. rotundus_, spread through persistent underground tubers that make them very difficult to eradicate.

## When rice is most vulnerable

First 30 to 45 days after transplanting. Nutsedge is a season-long problem because of tuber sprouting.

## How sedges show in satellite data

- **Uneven low-canopy zones** at establishment - sedges add greenness but rarely reach the height of rice.
- Same zones affected season after season because tubers persist for years - visible as chronic low-yield patches in seasonal-analysis reports.
- Poor separation from grass weeds and nutrient deficiency in imagery alone.

<Warning>
  Nutsedge tubers survive drying, tillage, and most single herbicide applications. Managing it is a multi-season effort, not a one-time spray decision.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- Triangular stems (grasses have round or flat stems) - the field diagnostic test
- V-shaped leaves in three ranks (not two, as in grasses)
- Purple nutsedge: dark green leaves with a shiny surface; underground tubers
- Umbrella-like flower clusters at maturity
- Sedges thrive in poorly leveled fields with alternating wet and dry patches

## Reduce the damage by

- **Level the field well** and maintain 3 to 5 cm standing water to suppress sedge germination.
- **Prevent sedge seed set** - hand-remove flowering sedges before they seed.
- **Deplete nutsedge tubers** through repeated tillage in dry fallow (each tillage forces a tuber to sprout, using up its reserves).
- **Pre-emergence herbicide** - pretilachlor \+ safener, oxadiargyl, or pyrazosulfuron - within 3 to 5 days of transplanting.
- **Post-emergence herbicide** - bensulfuron-methyl, ethoxysulfuron, or 2,4-D (grass rice safe rate) for broadleaf sedges; MCPA for nutsedge.
- **Do not rely on a single herbicide** for repeated seasons on nutsedge; rotate modes of action.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Because sedge zones are chronic, the highest-value alert is a _seasonal comparison_ rather than a within-season trigger.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for sedges on rice:

- **Seasonal analysis** - identify chronic low-yield zones with sedge history and target them with pre-emergence herbicide next season.
- **NDVI trajectory alert** at 15 to 30 days after transplanting on flagged sedge-prone fields.
