Stem borer larvae tunnel inside the rice stem, cutting off water and nutrient supply to the tiller. At vegetative stage, this kills the central growing point and produces a **deadheart** (dead central tiller pulled out easily). At reproductive stage, it cuts the panicle base and produces a **whitehead** (pale, empty panicle standing above the crop).

The main species are yellow stem borer (_Scirpophaga incertulas_), striped stem borer (_Chilo suppressalis_), pink stem borer (_Sesamia inferens_), and dark-headed stem borer (_Chilo polychrysus_). Management is similar across species.

## When rice is most vulnerable

Entire vegetative to reproductive period. Vegetative-stage damage causes deadhearts; reproductive-stage damage causes whiteheads. Whiteheads cause greater yield loss because the tiller is no longer replaceable.

## How stem borers show in satellite data

- **Whiteheads at heading** appear as small pale flecks and, in heavy infestations, as reduced field-scale NDVI at the reproductive stage.
- Deadhearts at tillering do not usually cross the detection threshold from satellite - the plant compensates with new tillers.
- Sudden pale patches after heading, with no weather cause, are a stem-borer signal.

<Warning>
  Whiteheads look the same from above whether caused by stem borer, neck blast, drought at flowering, or sheath rot. Ground confirmation is essential.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- **Deadheart** (vegetative stage): central tiller dries out and can be pulled out easily; the rest of the plant looks normal
- **Whitehead** (reproductive stage): whole panicle is pale, dry, empty, and stands above the healthy crop
- Larval tunnels visible when the stem is split open
- Frass (insect droppings) in the tunnels
- Egg masses on leaves - flat cream/brown patches near the leaf tip

## Reduce the damage by

- **Grow moderately resistant varieties** where available.
- **Destroy egg masses** during weeding and hand-remove infested tillers.
- **Synchronized planting** and destruction of stubble between seasons reduces the carryover population.
- **Preserve natural enemies** - egg parasitoids (Trichogramma) provide substantial free control. Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides.
- **Release Trichogramma cards** where they are available and locally validated.
- **Selective insecticide** (chlorantraniliprole or cartap) only if scouting shows the economic threshold is crossed. Apply based on egg-mass counts, not calendar.
- **Do not spray at heading** to protect pollinators and beneficials.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Focus alerts on the reproductive stage where whiteheads become detectable.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for stem borers on rice:

- **NDVI drop alert** during heading - trigger on a drop of 0.08 or more.
- **New Imagery alert** during the two weeks around heading so early whitehead patches are caught quickly.
