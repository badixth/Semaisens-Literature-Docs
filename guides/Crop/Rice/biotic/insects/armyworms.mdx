Armyworms (_Mythimna separata_, _Spodoptera mauritia_, and related species) are episodic pests. When they arrive they arrive in outbreak numbers and can completely defoliate a rice field in a few nights. They are voracious nocturnal feeders and hide in the base of the canopy or in soil cracks during the day.

## When rice is most vulnerable

Any stage from seedling through tillering, and again at grain filling. Late-instar larvae cause the majority of the damage in a very short time.

## How armyworms show in satellite data

- **Very rapid, large NDVI drops between consecutive passes** - often 0.20 or more in less than a week.
- Pattern is often **field-scale and follows the direction of larval movement** across the crop.
- Different from drought (slow) and disease (usually with a discoloration pattern) - armyworm damage is fast and mechanical.

<Warning>
  Armyworm damage moves faster than the imagery cycle. A weekly satellite pass may catch it too late. Local scouting, moth traps, and neighborhood outbreak reports remain the earliest signals.
</Warning>

## Field symptoms

- Leaves chewed from the margin inward, often overnight
- Only leaf midribs remain in severe defoliation
- Larvae found in the base of the canopy, in soil cracks, or under debris during the day
- Panicles cut at the base in reproductive-stage attacks
- Frass (dark green droppings) at the plant base

## Reduce the damage by

- **Encourage bird predation** - ducks in the field between crops eat larvae and pupae.
- **Flood the field temporarily** to force larvae out of cracks and expose them to predators.
- **Hand-collect and destroy** where practical on small holdings.
- **Selective insecticide** (chlorantraniliprole, emamectin benzoate, or spinosad) applied late afternoon or evening when larvae are active - only if scouting confirms an active outbreak.
- **Grow moderately resistant cultivars** where available.
- **Community-level response** - armyworm outbreaks are regional; coordinated action across neighboring fields is more effective than individual response.

## Set an alert for early warning

<Tip>
  Turn the diagnosis into a trigger. Armyworm outbreaks move fast; combine imagery, weather, and pheromone-trap information.
</Tip>

Recommended settings for armyworms on rice:

- **NDVI drop alert** - trigger on any drop of 0.15 or more between consecutive passes.
- **New Imagery alert** during outbreak season so a sudden change is reviewed within hours.
- **Regional outbreak reports** - subscribe to national extension armyworm bulletins if available in your area.
