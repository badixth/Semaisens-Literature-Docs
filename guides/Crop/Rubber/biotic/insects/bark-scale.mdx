**Scale insects** (various species) and **mealybugs** occasionally infest young rubber stands, feeding on stems and branches. Damage is usually minor and self-correcting because natural enemies (ladybird beetles, parasitoid wasps) keep populations in check. Chemical intervention is only needed on severe immature-block outbreaks.

## Cause

- **Immature blocks** (year 1 to 4) with tender bark
- **Ant activity** protecting scales/mealybugs from natural enemies
- **Dry weather** favoring scale reproduction
- **Neighboring host crops** (some scales are polyphagous)

## Effect

- Small brown, grey, or white scales attached to young stems and branches
- Sooty mold on leaves (from honeydew)
- Reduced growth on heavily infested saplings
- Rarely fatal but delays canopy build

## Satellite signals

None at satellite scale.

## Action

<Steps>
  <Step title="Inspect and estimate coverage">
    Look for scales on the underside of young branches and stems. Estimate percent of stem surface covered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Encourage natural enemies">
    Do not spray broad-spectrum insecticides unless coverage exceeds ~30%. Ladybird beetles and parasitoid wasps usually clean up the infestation within 4 to 8 weeks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Control ants">
    If ants are protecting the scales, sticky bands on the trunk or targeted ant baits break the association and let natural enemies work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Chemical option (severe cases only)">
    On year 1-3 blocks with \> 30% stem coverage: **horticultural oil** or **buprofezin** (insect growth regulator). Avoid neonicotinoids on flowering cover crops.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Recommended alert settings

| Trigger | Condition | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scout report | \> 30% stem coverage on year 1-3 sapling | Medium |
| Scout report | Sooty mold on canopy | Low (natural-enemy monitoring) |
