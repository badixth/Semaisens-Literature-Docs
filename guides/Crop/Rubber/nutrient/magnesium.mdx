---
title: "Magnesium Deficiency in Rubber"
sidebarTitle: "Magnesium (Mg)"
description: "How magnesium deficiency shows on older rubber leaves, Whorl 2 tissue thresholds, and the kieserite / dolomite program for acid tropical soils."
---

Magnesium is the central atom of chlorophyll. Deficiency shows first on older leaves (Mg is mobile, remobilized to new growth) as yellowing with characteristic red-purple margins. Common on very acid soils (pH \< 4.5), sandy soils, and blocks with heavy K fertilization (K and Mg compete for uptake).

## Cause

- **Very acid soils** (pH \< 4.5)
- **Sandy soils** with low Mg reserves
- **Heavy K fertilization** without Mg balance (K:Mg ratio \> 3:1 in soil)
- **Coastal lowland** and peat soils
- **Erosion** on hilly blocks

## Effect

Field signs:

- Older leaves in the lower whorl yellow first (Mg is mobile)
- Yellowing progresses to red-purple **leaf margins** (classic signature)
- Interveinal chlorosis: yellow between veins, veins stay green
- Newer whorls stay normal until deficiency is severe
- Premature leaf drop in extreme cases

## Leaf-tissue thresholds

Sample **Whorl 2** leaves.

| Mg level (% dry weight) | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| \< 0.20 | Deficient |
| 0.20 to 0.24 | Marginal |
| 0.24 to 0.30 | Optimal |
| \> 0.30 | High |

<Warning>
  This is a **generic band for the crop**, with no site, soil, clone, age or season qualifier. `Deficient` and `Optimal` resolve. **`Marginal` does not** — that tier exists because the transition is not sharp. **`High` does not either**, because no page states what High means or what to do about it. And where more than one nutrient reads deficient, **only the most deficient rating is valid.** See [Reading a tissue analysis](/snippets/tissue-diagnosis).
</Warning>

Also check K:Mg ratio in soil; should be 2:1 to 3:1.

## Satellite signals

Mg deficiency is patchy across the canopy (lower whorl only) and does not have a strong satellite signature until severe. Rely on ground scouting and tissue analysis.

## Action

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm with tissue and soil test">
    Whorl 2 Mg \< 0.20% and soil Mg \< 0.5 me/100g confirms deficiency.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply kieserite or magnesium sulphate">
    Kieserite (27% MgO, 22% S) is the standard fast-acting Mg source:

    - Immature: 100 to 200 g kieserite/tree/year
    - Mature: 200 to 300 g kieserite/tree/year
  </Step>
  <Step title="Long-term correction with dolomite">
    Ground magnesium limestone (dolomite, ~20% MgO, ~30% CaO) raises pH and supplies Mg. Apply 500 kg to 1 t/ha every 3 to 5 years on acid soils.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebalance K:Mg">
    If heavy KCl programs are causing induced Mg deficiency, split K applications more finely and interleave with Mg.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow up">
    Re-sample Whorl 2 leaves 4 months after correction. New whorls should be normal green; older leaves may not recover.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Recommended alert settings

| Trigger | Condition | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaf tissue Mg | \< 0.20% on scheduled sample | Medium |
| Soil pH | \< 4.5 on scheduled soil test | Medium |
| Soil K:Mg ratio | \> 4:1 | Medium |
| Scout report | Red-purple margins on lower-whorl leaves | Medium |
