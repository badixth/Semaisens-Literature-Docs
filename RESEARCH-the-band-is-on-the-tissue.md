# The band exists. It is on the plant, not on the bag.

**Status: research, and it reshapes the open decision.** Two parallel searches, 11 August 2026, against Malaysian primary sources with international corroboration separated out. Every quotation is verbatim; every gap is named.

`DECISION-rate-bands-do-not-exist.md` asked *how a band is derived from a point recommendation*. **That question has an answer, and it is that we were looking at the wrong quantity.**

---

## 1 · The finding, in one line

> **Nobody publishes a min/max on the rate. Everybody publishes a min/max on the nutrient concentration in the plant — and there are published functions from that concentration to a rate.**

The guardrail we specified validates the wrong thing. It asks *is this rate inside a band*, and no such band exists in any literature reachable from Malaysia. The agronomy asks *is this plant inside its band*, publishes that band with two numbers, and then computes the rate from the distance.

**This is the same architecture the competitor uses** — Terra Oracle measures soil, compares to a sufficiency level, and derives the rate inverse to the deficit. Malaysian oil palm agronomy does it with **leaf tissue** rather than soil, which is a stronger instrument for a perennial.

---

## 2 · The oil palm tissue band, with numbers

**Source:** I.R. Rankine and T.H. Fairhurst, *Field Handbook: Oil Palm Series, Volume 3 — Mature*, 2nd ed., Potash & Phosphate Institute, Singapore, 1999. Reproduced as Tables 4 and 5 of the *Smallholder Oil Palm Handbook, Module 4*, Woittiez et al., 3rd ed., August 2016.

Frond 17, palms over six years:

| Nutrient | Deficient | **Good** | Excessive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N, % DM | < 2.30 | **2.40 – 2.80** | > 3.00 |
| P, % DM | < 0.14 | **0.15 – 0.19** | > 0.25 |
| K, % DM | < 0.75 | **0.90 – 1.20** | > 1.60 |
| Mg, % DM | < 0.20 | **0.25 – 0.40** | > 0.70 |
| B, mg/kg | < 8.0 | **15 – 25** | > 40 |
| Cu, mg/kg | < 3.0 | **5.0 – 8.0** | > 15 |
| Zn, mg/kg | < 10 | **12 – 18** | > 20 |

**Look at the shape of that table.** Three classes, a middle band, and an explicit **Excessive** class above it. That is nominal-is-best, published, in the crop's own vocabulary — **the same structure this product already uses for its index bands.** The mental model was right; it was pointed at the wrong measurement.

**And it bands by palm age.** Palms 1–6 years carry different numbers — K optimum **1.10 – 1.30** against the mature **0.90 – 1.20**. Reading a young stand against the mature band reproduces the young-mature error already on our record, one level down in the nutrient domain.

**The action on over-supply is published too:** *"If the leaf concentration of a specific nutrient is excessive, the application of this nutrient fertiliser should be reduced… In general, a reduction of applied quantities by a quarter or a third is recommended."*

## 3 · Four published derivations from deficit to rate — three of them Malaysian

**Primary source:** Goh, K.J., *Fertilizer Recommendation Systems for Oil Palm: Estimating the Fertilizer Rates*, Applied Agricultural Research Sdn. Bhd., Sg. Buloh, Selangor. 37 pp. **No year printed**; internal evidence places it at 2004 or later.

**3.1 · How the two limits of a band are defined.** This is the derivation pattern in its purest form:

> *"Foster et al. (1988) set the **upper limit for leaf critical level at the yield response of 0.5 t/ha/year** and **lower limit at 1.5 t/ha/year**."*

The bounds are not chosen. They are the concentrations at which modelled yield response crosses a stated tonnage. **A band with a reason.**

**3.2 · The IRHO adjustment equation** — deficit straight to rate, and it goes negative on over-supply:

> `Dn = Dn-1 + a(Nn-1 – Nn) + b(Nc – Nn)`

Fitted at Kunak, Sabah: a = −10.67, b = 12.84, r = 0.45. Worked: leaf N 2.53% against Nc 2.65% → **+2.07 kg**. Leaf N 2.73% → **−0.49 kg**.

**3.3 · Foster's adjustment table** — and note the adjustment is itself a range:

| Rating | Yield response, t/ha/yr | Fertiliser adjustment, kg/palm/yr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Excessive | 0 | **−0.5 to −1** |
| Satisfactory | 0 – 1 | 0 |
| Low | 1 – 2 | **0 to 1** |
| Deficient | 2 – 3 | **0 to 2** |
| Very deficient | > 3 | **0 to 3** |

**Deviation in either direction moves the rate, and it moves it downward above the band.** Nominal-is-best, in the rate domain, published.

**3.4 · INFERS**, Kee, Goh, Chew & Tey, 1994, ISP Planters' Conference — splits demand into growth and deficiency, and carries **a hard physiological ceiling**:

> *"to prevent over manuring, INFERS has set a **maximum N uptake rate of 1180 g per palm per year**."*

## 4 · MPOB does publish ranges — on peat

Hasnol Othman, MPOB Agronomy & Geospatial Unit, MPOB–SOPPOA Workshop, Sibu, 10 March 2016:

> *"The optimum rate of N fertilizer application is **less than 1.0 kg palm⁻¹ yr⁻¹ of urea (0.5 to 0.6 kg)**."*
> *"The optimum rate of P fertilizer for oil palm on peat **should not exceed 1.0 kg RP palm⁻¹ yr⁻¹**."*
> *"The optimum rate of K fertilizer for oil palm on peat recommended at **4.0 – 6.0 kg MOP palm⁻¹ yr⁻¹**."*

**A min/max on K, a ceiling on P, a ceiling plus interval on N — from MPOB, for a named soil group.** The P ceiling has a pathological reason (Ganoderma basal stem rot); the N ceiling an emissions one.

**So the flat claim that no Malaysian authority publishes a range is now false, and should be corrected.** It holds for mineral soils, where every schedule retrieved is points. It does not hold for peat.

## 5 · Rice — the lookup existed, and DOA deleted it

The record needs correcting here too. `DECISION-rate-bands-do-not-exist.md` says the LCC-to-rate lookup is not published. **It was published, in 2008, and removed from the 2022 successor.**

**Jabatan Pertanian Malaysia, *Pakej Teknologi Padi*, 2008, ISBN 978-983-047-139-6**, Jadual 11 and 12:

> *"Tahap kritikal, Leaf Color Chart (LCC) No.3 atau bacaan SPAD kurang 32"*
> *"Baja Urea (Tambahan) … **50 kg/ha (luar musim) atau 75 kg/ha (musim utama)**"*

**The 2022 *Rice Check: Padi* contains "LCC" five times and not one number** — every instance defers to the app. And the Play Store link on DOA's own live page returns 404.

**Four caveats that must travel with the 2008 figures:**

- **The rate is kg of urea product, not kg N.** The document never prints urea's N content.
- **The chart's panel count is never stated.** Shade 3 on a 4-panel chart is a different colour from shade 3 on a 6-panel chart.
- **Variety-blind.** MR263 and MR269 do not appear in the document.
- **Two Malaysian sources disagree.** IADA Pulau Pinang uses **No. 3 direct-seeded and No. 4 transplanted**, and prints 76 rather than 75 kg/ha.

**It is a trigger with a point dose, not a band.** Internationally the band form exists — Bijay-Singh et al. 2012 gives 45 / 30 / 0 kg N/ha across `<4`, `4–5`, `≥5` — but **that requires a 6-panel chart, and shade 5 does not exist on the 4-panel IRRI chart.**

**One thing worth encoding regardless:** Main season gets **more** nitrogen than off-season — MARDI +15%, DOA +50% on the LCC top-up — and the reason is **leaching from higher rainfall, not the yield gap.** Main season yields less and gets more. Any interface implying *more fertiliser → more yield* reads backwards here.

## 6 · So the base model has three layers, not one

**The schema we specified is the wrong shape.** `product_bands` keyed on crop × product asks for a number that is not published anywhere. Replace it with:

| Layer | What it holds | Status today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Tissue band** | crop × nutrient × age → deficient / good / excessive, with units and a citation. **This is where the min/max lives.** | **Oil palm: available now**, ten nutrients, two age classes. **Rice: a threshold, not a band.** |
| **2 · Derivation** | crop → a published function from deficit to rate, with its coefficients and its source | **Oil palm: four published**, three Malaysian. **Rice: DOA's trigger + dose.** |
| **3 · Attribution** | who chose the parameters the literature leaves open | **PR #5** |

**Layer 3 does not disappear, and this is the honest part.** Foster's system computes a full yield surface and then picks the rate **by economic criterion**:

> *"Foster (1995) assumed that the larger plantation companies can afford to take **higher risk (20% return)** … compared with **smallholders who will take lower risk (100% return)**."*

**The agronomy is derivable and citable. The risk appetite is a business policy and always will be.** So `DECISION-rate-bands-do-not-exist.md`'s question — *who owns the derivation* — is **narrowed, not dissolved.** PR #5 remains necessary. It now covers a much smaller surface.

## 7 · Two rules the product must implement, or it will fabricate

**7.1 · A multi-nutrient diagnosis is not a set of independent verdicts.** Goh, after Foster 2003:

> *"if any nutrient is found to be **very deficient**, or **more than one** nutrient is deficient, then the deficiency rating of **only the most deficient nutrient is considered to be valid**… This implies that the system **only works if the nutritional state of the palm is near the optimum**."*

**Rendering five nutrient verdicts side by side when three read deficient is a fabricated claim of the same family as averaging a band.** The correct behaviour is to name the single most deficient nutrient and **refuse the rest**, with the reason.

**7.2 · The band is site-specific, and a single global band per crop reintroduces the collapse.** Foster's computed K band at one site is **0.77 – 0.86%**; the generic mature band is **0.90 – 1.20%**. They disagree **by design**:

> *"the optimum nutrient concentration varies substantially between soil types, terrain, palm age, climate, season, frond age, sampling methods… simplistic or careless application of foliar analysis will produce misleading results."*

A tissue band carries its scope exactly as an index band carries its stage.

## 8 · The provenance problem, stated plainly

**The canonical leaf table is PPI/IPNI's, not MPOB's.** MPOB invokes "the critical level" as a known standard in Bulletin 71, and shades an *"Optimum levels"* band on its own charts in the 2016 peat deck, **without printing the bounds anywhere reachable.**

PPI is a fertiliser-industry institute. That is the same class of provenance concern that got the per-palm borate figures refused — better sourced by a distance, but **not a Malaysian authority.** Under the schema above it would be `sourced` with the citation naming Rankine & Fairhurst, and an agronomist could promote or replace it.

## 9 · What to chase next, in order

1. **OCR MPOB Bulletin 72.** The PDF is live at `palmoilis.mpob.gov.my/publications/OPB/opb72-afandi.pdf` — **no longer 404** — but it has no text layer. Index snippets show a Table 4 classifying nutrients **very low to very high** and a Table 5 on nutrient balance. **This is the single most valuable unread document**, and it may contain MPOB's own bands.
2. **The FFTC 2018 paper**, `doi.org/10.56669/fvyg4353` — *"Location specific fertilizer management for rice using soil test target yield approach in Malaysia"*. **Paywalled**, and the most likely home of the rice calibration equations.
3. **MS 2530:2022**, paywalled via MySOL. Would settle whether any buffer-zone or rate rule is regulatory rather than agronomic.
4. **`ebuletin.mardi.gov.my` is entirely down** — NXDOMAIN. MARDI's bulletin content survives only in Wayback, and Bil. 48 has **no capture at all**.

## 10 · What this does not change

**Keep VRA refusing today.** Nothing here is loaded into the platform, and the product holds no leaf analysis at all — our inputs are NDVI, NDRE, NDMI and SAR.

**The bridge is the open question, and it is the right one.** NDRE responds to leaf chlorophyll and therefore to leaf nitrogen, and the leaf-nitrogen band is now in hand with numbers. **Whether NDRE can be calibrated to leaf N% on Malaysian oil palm is a research question with a real answer**, unlike *"what is the min/max rate"*, which had none. It needs local calibration and it must not be assumed.

**That reframes the platform's own gap honestly:** we cannot derive a rate from an index today. We can say precisely what would let us — a leaf analysis, or a calibration from NDRE to leaf N — and both are obtainable, which the rate band never was.
