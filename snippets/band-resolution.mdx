A **healthy NDVI band** is a definition, not a measurement. It resolves against a fixed set of keys, and every key must be supplied. Drop one and the result is not a wider band - it is no band at all.

## The keys

| Crop | A band resolves against |
| --- | --- |
| Paddy | crop × **ecosystem** × **phase** (days after establishment) |
| Oil palm | crop × **planting environment** × **stand age** |
| Rubber | crop × **planting environment** × **stand age** |
| Pineapple | crop × **planting environment** × **phase** (months after planting) × ratoon cycle |

## Why there is no summary band

Earlier revisions of the ecosystem pages carried two shortcuts: a _typical peak NDVI_ figure per environment, and an environment-independent table of bands by phase or stand age. Both have been removed, because **neither resolves to a block.**

A _typical peak_ collapses the phase axis: it answers "what does this environment reach at its best", which is not a question any block asks on any given day. An environment-independent phase table collapses the environment axis, and that one is worse - the numbers have to come from somewhere, and in practice they came from the best-drained environment. Every other environment then reads as underperforming.

A worked example. A 10-year-old oil palm block on inland peat, reading **0.71**:

| Read against | Band | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inland peat, 6 - 15 years | 0.68 - 0.82 | In band. Normal. |
| Environment-independent, 6 - 15 years | 0.75 - 0.88 | Below band. Looks deficient. |
| "Typical peak", inland peat | 0.65 - 0.82 | In band. Normal. |

One block, one reading, three answers - and the middle one was the table the page told you to use first. The block was never underperforming; it was being read against a mineral-upland expectation.

<Warning>
  This is the same error as reading a young stand against a prime-mature band, one axis over. A stand that has not closed canopy is not deficient, it is young. A block on peat is not deficient, it is on peat.
</Warning>

## What to do instead

Set the block's **planting environment** and its **planting or establishment date** in block settings. The platform derives the phase or stand age and resolves the one band that applies. The Health tab legend switches to match.

Where a key cannot be resolved - no environment set, no date, or a scope spanning several environments or age classes - the platform says which key is missing. It does not fall back to a wider band, and it does not average the bands that would have applied.

<Note>
  A scope that spans several bands has a **distribution**, not a band. Where several resolve, each is named with the ground it covers rather than merged into one range.
</Note>

## A reading above the band

The band describes what healthy looks like at this phase in this environment. **It is not a floor.**

A reading **above** its band is a finding, not the absence of one. It says the block is not doing what this phase expects, and on every crop the usual causes are things other than vigour: vegetation that is not the crop, a wrong planting date, or a stage that has not arrived when the calendar says it should. The crop pages name the specific causes.

This does not make an above-band reading a problem by itself. It makes it **a question the platform should ask**, in a place where it currently asks nothing.

<Warning>
  **Saturation carve-out.** Above the point where an index stops discriminating, a higher reading does not mean a healthier canopy. Two blocks at 0.86 and 0.91 may be identical, or the second may be worse.

  The platform raises no above-band finding on a saturated reading, and **says a reading has saturated before naming its band**. A saturated figure ranks against nothing.
</Warning>

This carve-out is not a small exception. On oil palm and rubber it covers most of the productive life of the stand, which is why those two crops get the least from this rule and paddy and pineapple get the most.

NDRE keeps discriminating past the point where NDVI saturates. **This documentation publishes no NDRE band for any crop**, so there is currently nothing to read a post-saturation value against — an above-band question on a closed canopy is a question for a scout, not for the index. That gap is known and is not closed here.
