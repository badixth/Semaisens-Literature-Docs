Find quick answers to the most common questions about how Google works, what it supports, and how your data is managed. If you cannot find what you are looking for here, reach out to the team at [support@example.com](mailto:support@example.com) or use the in-app chat widget.

<Accordion title="How often is satellite imagery updated for my fields?">
  Google ingests Sentinel-2 imagery on a **5-day revisit cycle** under
  cloud-free conditions, which is the standard revisit frequency for the
  Sentinel-2 constellation at mid-latitudes. Planet and other commercial
  imagery providers available on Pro and Enterprise plans can deliver daily or
  near-daily revisits for your fields. Your imagery history and the date of the
  most recent acquisition are always visible in the field timeline view.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens when there is heavy cloud cover over my field?">
  When cloud cover obscures a field during a satellite pass, Google
  automatically skips that acquisition and flags the gap in your field
  timeline. The platform uses a configurable cloud cover filter
  (default: 20%) — any scene with cloud cover above that threshold over your
  field is excluded from analysis. You can adjust the threshold in
  **Settings → Preferences → Cloud Cover Filter**. Synthetic Aperture Radar
  (SAR) imagery, which penetrates cloud cover, is available as a supplementary
  source on Enterprise plans.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Which crops does the platform support?">
  Google supports all major row crops (corn, soybeans, wheat, cotton, canola),
  specialty crops (vegetables, tree fruits, nuts, vines), and pasture and
  forage grasses. Vegetation indices such as NDVI and NDRE are crop-agnostic
  and work across all plant types. Crop-specific stress models and growth stage
  tracking are available for corn, soybeans, wheat, and cotton. If you grow a
  crop not yet covered by a dedicated model, the core index analysis still
  applies — contact support to request a new crop model.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the minimum field size for analysis?">
  The minimum supported field size is **1 hectare (approximately 2.5 acres)**.
  Fields smaller than 1 hectare can be created and stored in Google, but they
  will not be processed for imagery analysis or vegetation index calculation.
  This limit exists because the spatial resolution of Sentinel-2 imagery
  (10 m per pixel) does not provide statistically reliable zonal statistics for
  very small areas.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I upload my own drone imagery?">
  Yes. Google accepts drone-captured orthomosaic imagery in GeoTIFF format.
  Navigate to **Fields → [Field Name] → Upload Imagery**, select your GeoTIFF
  file, and choose the bands present in your capture (RGB, multispectral, or
  thermal). Google processes the upload and calculates supported vegetation
  indices from the available bands. Drone imagery uploads count against your
  monthly storage quota — check your current usage in **Settings → Billing**.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I cancel my subscription?">
  You can cancel your subscription at any time from **Settings → Billing →
  Manage Subscription → Cancel Plan**. Your access continues until the end of
  the current billing period, after which your account reverts to the Free
  tier. Your field data and historical analysis are retained on the Free tier;
  features exclusive to paid plans become unavailable. If you want to delete
  your account and all associated data entirely, contact
  [support@example.com](mailto:support@example.com).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does the platform work outside the US?">
  Yes. Google supports fields in any country where Sentinel-2 imagery is
  available, which covers the entire globe. Platform language is currently
  English only, but coordinates, units (metric or imperial), and date formats
  can be configured per account in **Settings → Preferences**. Some
  third-party integrations — such as John Deere Operations Center organization
  sync — may be subject to regional restrictions imposed by the integration
  partner.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What coordinate reference system are coordinates in?">
  All coordinates in Google are expressed in **WGS84 (EPSG:4326)** — the
  same reference system used by GPS devices and most web mapping platforms.
  Exported GeoTIFF rasters use UTM projection (the appropriate zone for each
  field) by default, but you can select WGS84 geographic coordinates or a
  custom EPSG code in the export settings. Field boundary GeoJSON files always
  use WGS84 longitude/latitude.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I share field access with my agronomist?">
  Yes. Google supports role-based team access at the farm operation level.
  Navigate to **Settings → Team** and invite your agronomist, advisor, or
  consultant by email address. You can assign one of three roles: **Viewer**
  (read-only access to fields and analysis), **Scout** (can create and edit
  scouting observations), or **Manager** (full access except billing). Invited
  users receive an email with a link to join your organization — they do not
  need an existing Google account.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How long is my historical data retained?">
  Google retains all field data, imagery, and analysis results for the **life
  of your account** on Pro and Enterprise plans. On the Free tier, data older
  than 24 months may be archived and requires a plan upgrade to access. You
  can export all of your historical data at any time using the bulk export
  feature — see the [Data Export guide](/integrations/data-export) for
  instructions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is my farm data kept private?">
  Yes. Your farm data — including field boundaries, imagery, crop plans, and
  analysis results — is private to your account and any team members you
  explicitly invite. Google does not sell, share, or license your data to
  third parties, including input suppliers, grain traders, or other farmers.
  Data is encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (AES-256). You can review
  the full privacy policy at [example.com/privacy](https://example.com/privacy).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What imagery sources are used, and are they free?">
  Google ingests **Sentinel-2** imagery from the European Space Agency (ESA)
  at no additional cost on all plan tiers — Sentinel-2 is a publicly funded,
  freely available dataset. Pro and Enterprise plans also include access to
  **Planet PlanetScope** (3–5 m resolution, daily revisit) and **Airbus SPOT**
  imagery, which are commercial sources licensed by Google and included in your
  subscription cost. You are not billed separately for individual satellite
  scenes. Drone imagery uploaded by you uses your own licensed data and your
  plan's storage quota.
</Accordion>
